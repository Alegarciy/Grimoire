## Text Book Notes

### Analysis Goals
For the main goals when you analyze the models, you should keep in mind:
+ **Step I**: We are willing to accept that our conclusions are conditional on our particular dataset
+ **Step II**: We want our conclusions to remain valid when using another dataset generated by the same mechanism

Lets break down into smaller analysis achievements we desire for this:
+ **Step I**: We assume that it comes form the same distribution $\mathcal{D} \sim p_0(\cdot)$
  + Classifier Models
    - Step 0: Estimate the generalization error
    - Step 1: Compare two classifier models see if we can conclude if they perform different
  + Regression Models
    - Step 0: Estimate the generalization error
    - Step 1: Compare two regression models see if we can conclude if they perform different

### Notation & Formulas

#### Model & Error Terms

**$f_{\mathcal{D},A}$**
- Read: "f trained on dataset D, model A"
- Means: the prediction function you get after training model A on your data D
- Expanded: $f_{\mathcal{D},A}(\mathbf{x}) = \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^\top \mathbf{w}^*$

---

**$E^{\text{gen}}_{\mathcal{D},A}$**
- Read: "generalization error of model A trained on D"
- Means: expected loss of $f_{\mathcal{D},A}$ over all future data
- Expanded: $E^{\text{gen}}_{\mathcal{D},A} = \int p(\mathbf{x},y)\, L(f_{\mathcal{D},A}(\mathbf{x}), y)\, d\mathbf{x}\, dy$

---

**$N^{\text{test}}$**
- Read: "N test"
- Means: the number of observations in your test set
- Expanded: $N^{\text{test}} = |\mathcal{D}^{\text{test}}|$

---

**$z_i$**
- Read: "z sub i"
- Means: per-observation difference in loss between the two models on observation $i$
- Expanded: $z_i = L(f_{\mathcal{D},A}(\mathbf{x}_i), y_i) - L(f_{\mathcal{D},B}(\mathbf{x}_i), y_i)$

---

**$z_\mathcal{D}$**
- Read: "z given dataset D"
- Means: the true difference in generalization error between A and B
- Expanded: $z_\mathcal{D} = E^{\text{gen}}_{\mathcal{D},A} - E^{\text{gen}}_{\mathcal{D},B}$

---

**$\hat{z}_\mathcal{d}$**
- read: "z-hat given d"
- means: your *estimate* of $z_\mathcal{d}$ from finite test data
- expanded: $\hat{z}_\mathcal{d} = \frac{1}{n^{\text{test}}} \sum_{i=1}^{n^{\text{test}}} z_i$

---

**$D = (z_1, \ldots, z_n)$**
- Read: "D equals z one to z n"
- Means: the dataset that enters the statistical test, just the n per-observation differences
- Expanded: $D = (z_1, \ldots, z_n)$ where $z_i = L(f_{\mathcal{D},A}(\mathbf{x}_i), y_i) - L(f_{\mathcal{D},B}(\mathbf{x}_i), y_i)$

---

#### Statistical Terms

**$\theta$**
- Read: "theta"
- Means: the *true* difference in generalization error (uncomputable)
- Expanded: $\theta = E^{\text{gen}}_{A,\mathcal{D}} - E^{\text{gen}}_{B,\mathcal{D}}$

---

**$\hat{\theta}$**
- Read: "theta hat"
- Means: your estimated difference in generalization error between A and B
- Expanded: $\hat{\theta} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} z_i$

---

**$p_\theta(z_i)$**
- Read: "p theta of z i"
- Means: the probability distribution of observation $z_i$ governed by parameter $\theta$
- Expanded: $p(Z_i = z_i \mid \theta) = p_\theta(z_i)$

---

**$p_\theta(D)$**
- Read: "p theta of D"
- Means: the joint probability of observing the entire dataset, assuming independence
- Expanded: $p_\theta(D) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p_\theta(z_i)$

---

**$t_0(D)$** — Statistic: Mean
- Read: "t zero of D"
- Means: the sample mean, a function computed from the data
- Expanded: $t_0(D) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} Z_i$

---

**$t_1(D)$** — Statistic: Variance
- Read: "t one of D"
- Means: the sample variance, another function computed from the data
- Expanded: $t_1(D) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Z_i - t_0(D))^2$

---

### Hypothesis

**$H_0$**: $z_\mathcal{D} = 0$ — Model A and Model B have the same generalization error

**$H_1$**: $z_\mathcal{D} \neq 0$ — They differ

---

### Core Terms

**$r$**
- Read: "r"
- Means: proportion of disagreements where A is correct and B is wrong
- Expanded: $r = \frac{p_{12}}{p_{12} + p_{21}}$

---

**$s$**
- Read: "s"
- Means: total number of disagreements between the two classifiers
- Expanded: $s = n_{12} + n_{21}$

---

**$p(n_{12}, n_{21} \mid s)$**
- Read: "probability of n12 and n21 given s"
- Means: probability of observing this particular split of disagreements given the total number of disagreements
- Expanded: $p(n_{12}, n_{21} \mid s) = \binom{s}{n_{12}} r^{n_{12}}(1-r)^{n_{21}} = p_{\text{Binom}}(n_{12} \mid \theta = r, N = s)$

---

**$n_{11}$**
- Means: both A and B are correct
- Expanded: $n_{11} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i^A \cdot c_i^B$

---

**$n_{12}$**
- Means: A is correct, B is wrong
- Expanded: $n_{12} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i^A (1 - c_i^B)$

---

**$n_{21}$**
- Means: A is wrong, B is correct
- Expanded: $n_{21} = \sum_{i=1}^n (1 - c_i^A) c_i^B$

---

**$n_{22}$**
- Means: both A and B are wrong
- Expanded: $n_{22} = \sum_{i=1}^n (1 - c_i^A)(1 - c_i^B)$

---

**$c_i^A$**
- Read: "c sub i for model A"
- Means: whether model A correctly predicted observation $i$
- Expanded: $c_i^A = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \hat{y}_i^A = y_i \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

---

**$c_i^B$**
- Read: "c sub i for model B"
- Means: whether model B correctly predicted observation $i$
- Expanded: $c_i^B = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \hat{y}_i^B = y_i \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

### Expressing the p-values mathematically
The mathematical expression of a p-value is expressed in a complicated, letter dense way. But still is fun to have the expression so I decided to append it here:

> Assume each $z_i$ is normally distributed with unknown mean $\theta$ and known variance $\sigma_0^2$:
> $$p_\theta(z_i) = \mathcal{N}(z_i \mid \mu = \theta, \sigma^2 = \sigma_0^2)$$
>
> Under $H_0$ ($\theta = 0$), the mean estimator $t(D) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} z_i$ is distributed as:
> $$p_\theta(t(D) = t \mid H_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(t \mid \mu = 0, \sigma^2 = \frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}\right)$$
>
> The p-value (both tails) is then:
> $$p = 2\,\text{cdf}_\mathcal{N}\left(-|t_0| \;\middle|\; \mu = 0, \sigma^2 = \frac{\sigma_0^2}{n}\right)$$

### Evaluation of a single classifier with Jeffrey Prior
This is just a formula breakdown

> **Why the denominator becomes a Gamma fraction:**
>
> **Step 1 — Bayes:**
> $$p(\theta \mid m, n) = \frac{\overbrace{\text{Binom}(m \mid \theta, n)}^{\text{likelihood}} \cdot \overbrace{\text{Beta}(\theta \mid \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})}^{\text{prior}}}{\underbrace{\int_0^1 \text{Binom}(m \mid \theta', n) \cdot \text{Beta}(\theta' \mid \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})\, d\theta'}_{\text{normalizing constant}}}$$
>
> **Step 2 — Numerator kernel:**
> $$\text{Binom} \cdot \text{Beta} \propto \theta^{m - \frac{1}{2}}(1-\theta)^{n - m - \frac{1}{2}}$$
>
> **Step 3 — Recognize Beta shape:**
> $$\theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1} \quad \text{with} \quad a = m + \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad b = n - m + \tfrac{1}{2}$$
>
> **Step 4 — Denominator is the Beta normalizing constant:**
> $$\int_0^1 \theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1} d\theta = B(a,b) = \frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}$$
>
> **Step 5 — Final posterior:**
> $$p(\theta \mid m, n) = \frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+m)\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+n-m)}\,\theta^{\frac{1}{2}+m-1}(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}+n-m-1} = \text{Beta}(\theta \mid a, b)$$
>
> This is **conjugacy** — the prior and likelihood have matching shapes so the integral has a clean closed form instead of needing numerical computation.


### Example of p-values application

## Example: p-value from scratch

**Setup:** We have two models A and B tested on $n = 5$ observations.
We computed the per-observation differences $z_i$:

| $i$ | $z_i$ |
|-----|-------|
| 1   | 0.2   |
| 2   | -0.1  |
| 3   | 0.3   |
| 4   | 0.1   |
| 5   | 0.2   |

**Step 1 — Compute $t_0$:**
$$t_0 = \frac{1}{5}(0.2 - 0.1 + 0.3 + 0.1 + 0.2) = \frac{0.7}{5} = 0.14$$

**Step 2 — Assume** $H_0$ is true, so $\theta = 0$, and suppose we know $\sigma_0^2 = 0.04$:
$$t(D) \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\mu = 0, \sigma^2 = \frac{0.04}{5}\right) = \mathcal{N}(0, 0.008)$$

**Step 3 — Compute p-value:**
$$p = 2\,\text{cdf}_\mathcal{N}\left(-|0.14| \mid \mu = 0, \sigma^2 = 0.008\right) \approx 0.12$$

**Step 4 — Conclusion:**
- $p = 0.12 > 0.05$ → **fail to reject $H_0$**
- Our observed $t_0 = 0.14$ is not rare enough under $H_0$
- We cannot conclude A and B perform differently
